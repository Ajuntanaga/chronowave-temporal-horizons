Where the cubic exponent meets its evidence

This is the module that decides the framework's central kinetic claim, and it is the reason to read it first rather than last. The law is dτ/dt = (1−β²)k with k on trial — GR's ½ against the cubic claim's ⅓. Module VI runs that trial at β² ≈ 1.7×10−10, where the two predictions differ by 3×10−11 and it takes a 145-satellite array and a careful regression to tell them apart. The gravitational form of the same law, dτ/dt = (1−rs/r)k, carries the identical 33% relative split — but onto a quantity physics has been measuring since 1959. In the weak field it gives z = 2k|Φ|/c², so every gravitational-redshift measurement ever made is a measurement of k, and k = ⅓ predicts every one of them coming in a third small. Four anchors below, combined: k̂ = 0.50000 ± 0.00004 — GR at 0.0σ, the cubic exponent at 4762σ, with Gravity Probe A managing it single-handed in 1976. There is exactly one way out and the framework has never said whether it takes it; the panel states it, and cannot choose it for you. The same dressing (1−R/2GM)−1 is the horizon term of the field equation in Module X, and scale symmetry st·sτ = s never breaks: what coordinate time stretches, proper time repays.

Four independent anchors spanning ten decades of field strength each publish a ratio of measured shift to the GR prediction, and that ratio over two is k̂ directly: nothing fitted, nothing circular. The one retreat left, a cubic exponent kinematically with GR's square root gravitationally, is closed by the geoid. Every clock on it ticks alike only if kkin = kgrav, and the residual between two geoid clocks is (kgrav − kkin)·Δ(v²)/c². Boulder to Braunschweig would show 8.5×10−14, which is 854× outside what caesium fountains allow and 85,000× outside optical clocks. The framework's own 2π shell sits at 1.02599 rs under GR and 1.00405 under the cubic claim, 6.4× further from the horizon, and on Earth an optical-lattice clock separates the two exponents on a 2.7 cm shelf.
